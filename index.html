MissingGuard

Claim-Evidence Consistency Engine (CECE) — a Human-in-the-Loop safeguard for missing-person case closures

"AI should not decide when a missing-person search ends. It should make sure the evidence supports the decision."

When a missing-person case is closed, the closure report makes a claim (e.g. "confirmed in person", "confirmed by phone"). MissingGuard cross-checks that claim against the evidence on file — call logs, photo/biometric ID, timestamps, locations, and other system records — and flags cases where the claim and the evidence don't actually line up. It never closes, reopens, or judges a case itself. It only surfaces inconsistencies and routes them to a human supervisor.

All case data below is synthetic (no real police records or personal data are used). This prototype was motivated by public reporting on falsified case-closure reviews in South Korea (Jeju, 2025–26) and demonstrates the detection logic described in the MissingGuard project plan.

AI Ethics

  • MissingGuard does not auto-close or auto-reopen cases — the final decision always stays with a human.
  • MissingGuard does not accuse an officer of lying — it only shows where records and evidence disagree.
  • Synthetic data only — no real personal information is used in this prototype.
  • Every flag comes with an explanation of which records conflict and why, not just a risk score.
  • Default action on a flag is "request human review", never automatic blocking.
